# FlowLocal exhibit build (env:exhibit)

A FlowLocal image for one installation: a single Patternflow panel sends MIDI over Wi-Fi, and a laptop running Ableton Live receives it over USB. This build does nothing else. There is no MQTT broker, Director, USB stick, SD card, TFT, weather or upstream Wi-Fi.

```
Patternflow panel ──Wi-Fi, RTP-MIDI──▶ FlowLocal (ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1 N16R8) ──USB cable──▶ laptop / Live
```

This build lives on the private branch `exhibit-2026`. The regular envs (`flowlocal`, `flowlocal-sd`) are unchanged.

## Hardware

| Jack on the DevKit | Used for |
|---|---|
| **USB** (native USB) | The laptop. It powers the board, and the board shows up as the USB-MIDI device **Patternflow Exhibit** |
| **COM** (USB-UART bridge) | Flashing and the serial log (115200 baud) only |

- Leave the **USB-OTG** solder jumper **open**. The laptop supplies VBUS on the USB jack.
- Attach nothing else: no stick, no SD card, no TFT, no battery.
- The USB jack is a MIDI device only. It has no serial port and cannot be used for flashing, so flash through COM.

## What it does

- **Hotspot:** SSID `PatternflowLocal`, password `YOUR_PF_WIFI_PASS` (WPA2), channel 1, 6 or 11 as picked at boot, or one the laptop's monitor chose (below), 20 MHz wide, IP `192.168.66.1`. It admits 3 stations at once: the panel, an operator phone and one spare. No slot is kept for the panel, so keep at most two other devices on the hotspot. A device that walks out of range without disconnecting keeps its slot for a while (not yet measured on hardware), so turn off Wi-Fi on phones you are not using. The SSID, password, IP and on/off state are compiled in. The regular dashboard's settings in FlowLocal's own NVS namespace are ignored, and the console cannot change them: `POST /api/ap` and `POST /api/sta` return 403. The build refuses to compile with the old kit password `patternflow`.
  - The Wi-Fi driver also keeps its own copy of the AP settings and starts the AP with that copy before the firmware applies the compiled one. This build saves the compiled settings there, so from the second boot on the AP starts with the exhibit network directly. On the first boot after an erase, the driver's default open network (`ESP_xxxxxx`) appears for about a second. On the first boot after a browser update from a regular FlowLocal image, the old `patternflow` network appears for about a second. Erasing the flash before flashing (below) avoids the second case. When the automatic channel differs from the one stored at the last boot, the AP starts on the old one and moves within milliseconds, before any station can join.
  - **Boot silence:** on every boot the AP stays off for 12 s (`FL_EXHIBIT_AP_START_DELAY_MS`), so a panel still associated with the previous boot notices the AP is gone (its beacon-loss check takes about 8 s) and joins afresh when it returns. Measured 2026-09-19: with the AP back after 0.9 s the panel's MIDI stayed dead for over two minutes; with 15 s of silence it had its session back 1 s after the AP returned. The USB-MIDI port and the PF monitor's replies do not wait for it.
- **Hotspot channel (automatic):** in the last 3 s of the boot silence, FlowLocal scans every channel once (active scan, hidden networks included, 150 ms per channel at most, in station mode: probe requests from the station's MAC, no beacon from the AP's) and starts the AP on whichever of 1, 6 and 11 the networks it heard crowd least. Each network adds to each candidate `(RSSI + 100, clamped 0..70) × overlap`, where overlap is 100, 75, 50, 25, 10 % for a network 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 channels away and 0 from 5 on; a 40 MHz network's secondary half (4 channels above or below its primary) counts at half weight. The lowest score wins; a tie keeps channel 6 (`FL_AP_CHANNEL`), then the lower channel. The scan cannot hold the AP up: one that fails, or has not finished after 3 s (`FL_EXHIBIT_CHANNEL_SCAN_MS`), is stopped and channel 6 is used. The log shows what was heard and the scores; for the home scan of 2026-09-25 (router and mesh on 9 at about -70 dBm, a neighbour on 8 at about -83 dBm), as the host test scores it: `[FlWifi] channel scan: 3 networks in … ms (ch8 x1 -83dBm, ch9 x2 -70dBm)` and `[FlWifi] channel scores ch1 0, ch6 2350, ch11 3425 -> ch 1 (auto)`. `/api/status` has `ap_channel`, `ap_channel_mode`, `ap_channel_choice` and `ap_channel_scan`; the PF monitor reply has `ch`, `chm` and `cs` (the scores, below).
  - The channel is picked once per boot. A hall that fills with other networks later keeps FlowLocal where it is until the next restart (the monitor's **reboot FlowLocal** command, its **Wi-Fi channel** command, or a power cycle).
  - **Channel from the laptop** (PF monitor command 13, below): the monitor can fix a channel, 1–13, or step to the next of 1, 6 and 11 after the current one, or go back to automatic. FlowLocal keeps the choice in its NVS (namespace `fl_exhibit`, key `ap_ch`: 0 automatic, 1–13 that channel), acks, and restarts 300 ms later; the usual 12 s boot silence (a panel still associated has to notice the AP is gone before it looks for it again) then starts the hotspot on the stored channel, on every boot until the monitor sends another choice (`chm=manual`, `why=cmd13` on the restart it asked for). The scan still runs in the silence, so its scores are there to compare, and the log says what it would have picked: `[FlWifi] channel 6 chosen from the laptop (PF monitor command 13, NVS)`, then `[FlWifi] channel scores ch1 0, ch6 2350, ch11 3425 -> ch 6 (manual, from the PF monitor; the scan would pick 1)`. If the Wi-Fi driver refuses to start the AP on the stored channel, that boot runs on the automatic choice instead (`chm=auto`, logged) and the choice stays stored. "Automatic" from the monitor, sent while already automatic, is a way to scan again. Erasing NVS (below) forgets the choice.
  - `FL_EXHIBIT_AP_CHANNEL` `1`–`13` fixes the channel and skips the scan (`chm=fixed`); `0` (default) is automatic. Compile-time. It wins over the monitor's choice: such a build refuses command 13 and ignores a choice already stored (the log says so).
  - The AP runs 20 MHz wide (the driver stores that, so later boots start with it): 1, 6 and 11 overlap no neighbour only at that width, and MIDI needs no more.
- **RTP-MIDI:** a session listener named `FlowLocal` on UDP 5004 (control) and 5005 (data). It never invites anyone. The panel invites `192.168.66.1`, using the same SSID and password on its side (`PF_WIFI_SSID` / `PF_WIFI_PASS`, `PF_MIDI_DEFAULT_HOST`). Two sessions at most; one panel is expected.
- **Session repair:** five members of the AppleMIDI 3.5.0 session class are specialised in `src/fl_midi_bridge.h` for this build, and one library timeout is shortened through its settings. The library files are unchanged, and a different library version stops the build.
  - FlowLocal's RTP SSRC is derived from the board's MAC, so it stays the same across reboots.
  - **Why a BY is sent or not.** Every BY FlowLocal sends carries its own SSRC, the same for all its sessions, and the panel's library ends every session it holds with that SSRC (it matches a BY by SSRC alone). A BY that reaches a device ends whatever session that device has with FlowLocal, including one it has only just opened. So FlowLocal never sends a BY to the address an invitation it is accepting comes from, and after each BY of its own it sends no BY to unknown sessions for 1 s (below).
  - **The panel restarts, FlowLocal stays up:** the panel's new invitation from the same address and port replaces its old session at once, without a BY. With the stock library, the old session later timed out with a BY that also ended the panel's new session, causing a second dropout. The same SSRC with a new initiator token (the panel started over) is treated the same.
  - **FlowLocal restarts, the panel stays up:** the panel keeps sending heartbeats and MIDI to a session FlowLocal no longer has. FlowLocal answers the first such packet with a BY, the panel ends that session and invites again at once. With the stock library, the panel kept sending into the void until its own heartbeat timeout gave up.
  - **FlowLocal's OK lost on the air:** the panel repeats its invitation (same SSRC and token) about a second later. While no data invitation has come for that session (so the panel cannot have had the OK), FlowLocal answers each repeat with the same OK again, byte for byte (`[midi] rtp invitation repeated by 192.168.66.2: the same OK again`, counted in `rtp_retries_answered`). With the stock library the repeat was ignored, and the panel re-sent into silence until it gave the invitation up (3 tries in the exhibit panel, then a new invitation). A repeat that arrives after the data invitation is a stale copy and stays unanswered. A repeated OK cannot make a second session: FlowLocal adds nothing to its list, and the panel's library looks an OK up by its token and only updates that one participant; the worst a late extra OK does there is repeat the data invitation (answered as before) and report the join twice, which the exhibit panel counts once, by SSRC (patternflow ba29402, 198abaa).
  - **Never NO:** FlowLocal holds two sessions at most. An invitation that finds both taken (after the replacements above) used to get NO, which the panel's library drops without a word; the panel then waited for its next try (20 s in the log of 2026-09-27). Now the session heard from least recently is ended and the invitation accepted (`[midi] rtp session list full: ended 192.168.66.4's session (heard 1500 ms ago) with a BY for a new invitation`, counted in `rtp_evicted`). The one ended gets a BY only when it is on another address than the invitation; on the same address it is dropped silently.
  - **Silent sessions end after 25 s:** a session FlowLocal has heard nothing from for `FL_EXHIBIT_SESSION_TIMEOUT_MS` (25 s; the library's own is 61 s) is ended with a BY and counted in `to` (`[midi] rtp session of 192.168.66.2 silent for 25001 ms: ended with a BY`). Heard means a heartbeat, MIDI (the library counted only heartbeats) or a repeated invitation. The panel's heartbeat comes every 10 s, so one lost heartbeat (20 s) never ends a session; two in a row do. A panel still alive but unheard gets the BY and invites again at once; a panel that is gone no longer holds a slot for a minute, and `ps` says so sooner. When another session is on the same address (a second app on one device), the silent one is dropped without a BY. The BY cannot end a newer session of the same panel: a newer invitation with its SSRC or from its address and port has already replaced the old session. The build refuses a timeout under 21 s (two heartbeats) or over the library's 61 s.
  - For 1 s after any BY of FlowLocal's own (the two above, and **reconnect panel**), heartbeats and MIDI from unknown sessions get no BY: the panel's packets still under way when it read the BY would otherwise draw a BY that could reach it after its next session has begun.
- **Forwarding:** Control Change, Program Change, Note On and Note Off go from the panel to USB on the same channel. Other message types are counted and dropped. Traffic flows one way only: nothing from USB reaches the panel, and MIDI Thru is off. The only messages FlowLocal adds are the program mirror and the knob re-send (below), and the PF monitor's status replies and command acks, which answer the laptop's SysEx (below).
- **Latency path:** a dedicated FreeRTOS task (`fl-midi`, core 1, priority 12) reads RTP and writes to USB every 1 ms. The Arduino `loop()` is not in that path. The task starts with USB, first thing at boot; it reads RTP only once the hotspot is up and the session listener open.
- **Program re-send:** the bridge sends the last Program Change it saw again, followed by the program mirror and the knob values (both below), once per event, when:
  - that Program Change could not be written (USB buffer full, or no laptop);
  - USB (re)connects, or the laptop wakes;
  - the laptop sends the **resync request**: Control Change 119 on MIDI channel 16, value 127 (bytes `BF 77 7F`; any value above 0 counts), sent to the **Patternflow Exhibit** output. The Live device **PF Exhibit Scenes** sends it when it loads and from its own button, so a restarted Live learns the current pattern without anyone touching the panel. Requests less than 250 ms apart count once. Every other message from the laptop, apart from the PF monitor's SysEx (below), is discarded;
  - someone presses **Send current pattern to Live** on the status page (`POST /api/resend`).

  Nothing else sends it again. The USB buffer keeps order, so once the newest Program Change is in it, Live will read that one last. On a full USB buffer the bridge waits up to 4 ms before it counts a message as dropped. Once writes have kept failing for 0.5 s (nobody reading), it stops waiting.
- **Program mirror:** right after every Program Change the bridge forwards or re-sends, it also sends Control Change 118 on MIDI channel 16 with the program number as the value (bytes `BF 76 <n>`; pattern 3 is `BF 76 03`, Origin is `BF 76 00`). This is the fallback for a Live that does not deliver Program Change to a Max for Live `[midiin]`: turn on the Live device's **Alt CC** switch (off by default). Its **Alt CC number** already defaults to 118, and it then reads that CC as a program. The mirror goes out on channel 16 whatever channel the Program Change used.
  - It is written, retried and counted like the Program Change it follows: a forwarded Program Change's mirror gets the same wait on a full USB buffer and counts in `tx` or `dropped`; a re-sent one's mirror gets one try per 250 ms like the re-send.
  - It never goes out ahead of its Program Change. If the Program Change could not be written, the mirror is owed with it and both go out together later. If only the mirror could not be written, the mirror alone is owed. A newer Program Change replaces whatever is owed.
  - `FL_EXHIBIT_PROGRAM_CC` sets the control number (default 118, `0` turns the mirror off) and `FL_EXHIBIT_PROGRAM_CC_CHANNEL` the channel (default 16). Both are compile-time. The build refuses control numbers above 119, and the resync request's control number (119) on any channel: Live can rewrite the channel of what it sends back to this port, so a mirror echoed back could otherwise ask for another re-send. The Live device's **Alt CC number** goes from 1 to 118, so a build that changes the control number needs that number set to match. The Live device's Alt CC matches the control number on any channel (Live is reported to merge a track's input onto channel 1 before the first device; not yet checked on hardware), so the channel setting does not have to match the device.
  - The exhibit panel sends everything on channel 1, and its only Control Changes are CC 24, 25 and 26 from K1–K3 (the exhibit feature removes K4 before MIDI sees it; the midi feature's range is 24–27), so CC 118 on this port comes only from FlowLocal, whatever channel Live shows it on.
- **Knob re-send:** the bridge keeps the last value of Control Change 24, 25 and 26 on MIDI channel 1 (the panel's K1–K3) that the panel sent since FlowLocal started, whether or not it reached the laptop. Every time it re-sends the last Program Change (the four cases under Program re-send), it sends those values right after the Program Change and its mirror, K1 first (bytes `B0 18 <v>`, `B0 19 <v>`, `B0 1A <v>`). A Live that restarts, reconnects or wakes then gets the knob positions with the pattern, so the parameters mapped to K1–K3 match the panel instead of waiting for someone to turn a knob.
  - Only these three controls on channel 1 are kept. CC 118 and 119, channel 16 and notes are never kept or replayed. A knob the panel has not sent since FlowLocal started is left out. With no Program Change seen, nothing is re-sent at all.
  - They are written and counted like the re-sent mirror: one try per 250 ms, counted in `tx` and `knob_resends`, never ahead of the Program Change or mirror they follow. If only part of a re-send fits in the USB buffer, the rest follows later in the same order, and what already went out is not repeated. The values are read when they are written, so they are the newest the panel sent.
  - If a Program Change from the panel arrives while a re-send is owed, it takes the owed Program Change's place, and the knob values still follow it.
  - A Program Change forwarded from the panel (a pattern switch) brings no knob values: in the exhibit panel one position per knob holds across patterns, so a switch does not move them. A knob value the panel sent that could not be written (USB buffer still full after 4 ms, or no laptop) is not sent again by itself; the next re-send event carries it.
  - The values are replayed exactly as the panel sent them, so they have to be absolute. The exhibit panel sends K1–K3 as absolute positions whatever its MIDI page says (`PF_MIDI_OUT_LANE_ABSOLUTE` in its exhibit bundle). A panel sending Relative steps would have its last step replayed.
  - Like the last Program Change, the values are kept across panel sessions: a panel that restarts or reconnects replaces them only by sending new ones. The exhibit panel sends its K1–K3 values when its session starts (`PF_MIDI_OUT_LANE_ABSOLUTE`), which refreshes them. If those messages are lost on Wi-Fi (`rtp_gaps`), or the panel runs firmware without that flag, every re-send until someone turns the knob replays the value from the earlier session. Turning that knob corrects it in FlowLocal and in Live.
  - A re-sent value puts a Live parameter where the knob is only if the Live set leaves that parameter to the knob. The bridge writes the Program Change and the knob values back to back; in which order Live applies them and the scene launch the Program Change triggers is up to Live. A scene tempo on a tempo mapped to K1, or clip automation on a parameter mapped to K1–K3, sets that parameter again at every scene launch, and a pattern switch sends no knob values that could undo it.
  - `FL_EXHIBIT_KNOB_RESEND` `0` turns it off. `FL_EXHIBIT_KNOB_CC_FIRST`, `FL_EXHIBIT_KNOB_CC_LAST` and `FL_EXHIBIT_KNOB_CC_CHANNEL` (24, 26, 1) choose the controls. All are compile-time. The build refuses a range that contains CC 118 or 119 or has more than 8 numbers, and channel 16.
- **PF monitor (status and repairs over SysEx, protocol v1):** a monitor on the laptop asks FlowLocal how the signal path is doing, and asks it to repair it, through the same **Patternflow Exhibit** port Live uses, so it needs neither the hotspot nor the status page.

  | Laptop to FlowLocal | Bytes | FlowLocal answers |
  |---|---|---|
  | status request | `F0 7D 50 46 01 F7` | status reply `F0 7D 50 46 02 <text> F7` |
  | reconnect panel | `F0 7D 50 46 10 52 43 F7` (`R` `C`) | ack `F0 7D 50 46 03 <text> F7` |
  | reboot panel | `F0 7D 50 46 11 52 50 F7` (`R` `P`) | ack |
  | reboot FlowLocal | `F0 7D 50 46 12 52 46 F7` (`R` `F`) | ack |
  | Wi-Fi channel | `F0 7D 50 46 13 43 48 <c> F7` (`C` `H`, then `<c>`: `00` automatic, `01`–`0D` that channel, `7F` the next of 1 / 6 / 11) | ack |

  `7D` is the MIDI manufacturer ID for non-commercial use, `50 46` is `PF`. The two bytes after a command are its guard, then its argument bytes (only 13 has one). `<text>` is printable ASCII (`0x20`–`0x7E`), `key=value` fields separated by `;`, at most 240 bytes. Readers ignore keys they do not know.
  - **Status reply.** Every key is always there, in this order; a value FlowLocal does not know is empty (or `-1` where the table says). Example, from the host test: `v=1;fw=flx-mon3;up=3725;ap=1;ch=1;chm=auto;cl=2;cm=3;ps=1;pip=192.168.66.2;prs=-47;hd=1834;ra=212;lp=5;kn=64,-,127;jt=3;jm=41;rx=5120;tx=6144;dr=0;se=2;to=1;rj=0;gp=3;rr=4;ud=0;sr=1862;hi=181234;rsn=poweron;bc=1;why=;ur=0;cs=1:0,6:24,11:35` (239 bytes). After an automatic restart the restart keys read, for example, `rsn=sw;bc=2;why=bridge_hung;ur=0`; after the monitor chose channel 6, `…;ch=6;chm=manual;…;rsn=sw;bc=8;why=cmd13;ur=0;cs=1:480,6:524,11:611` (a crowded hall 40 s after that restart, from the host test).

    | Key | Value |
    |---|---|
    | `v` | protocol version, `1` |
    | `fw` | FlowLocal build tag, `FL_EXHIBIT_MON_FW_TAG` (default `flx-mon3`; `flx-mon2` had no `cs`, no command 13 and never `chm=manual`, and refused an invitation to a full session list; `flx-mon1` had no `rsn`, `bc`, `why`, `ur`) |
    | `up` | FlowLocal uptime in seconds, from the 64-bit timer (no wrap after 49 days); a drop means FlowLocal restarted |
    | `ap` | hotspot running, `1` or `0`; `0` through the boot silence |
    | `ch` | the hotspot's channel; empty while it is down |
    | `chm` | how this boot's channel was chosen: `auto` (the boot scan), `fixed` (`FL_EXHIBIT_AP_CHANNEL`) or `manual` (the monitor's command 13, kept in NVS); right from the boot silence |
    | `cl` | stations on the hotspot now |
    | `cm` | the hotspot's station limit (3) |
    | `ps` | RTP sessions up (`peers`) |
    | `pip` | the panel's address (newest session, as `panel_ip`); empty with no session |
    | `prs` | RSSI in dBm of the hotspot station whose DHCP lease is `pip`; empty when unknown |
    | `hd` | ms since MIDI or a heartbeat from any session, `-1` never (`last_heard_age_ms`) |
    | `ra` | ms since MIDI from a session, `-1` never (`last_rx_age_ms`) |
    | `lp` | last Program Change from the panel, `-1` none (`last_program`) |
    | `kn` | K1–K3's last values (CC 24–26), `-` for a knob not sent yet (`knob_values`); empty when the knob re-send is off |
    | `jt` | RTP interarrival jitter in ms (RFC 3550), `-1` before two packets from the panel (`rtp_jitter_ms`) |
    | `jm` | how much later than the quickest RTP packet of the last minute the slowest one arrived, ms; `-1` with none in the last minute (`rtp_spread_ms`) |
    | `rx`, `tx`, `dr` | `rx`, `tx`, `dropped` |
    | `se` | sessions opened since boot (`sessions`) |
    | `to`, `rj`, `gp` | `rtp_timeouts` (sessions ended after 25 s unheard), `rtp_rejected` (always `0` from `flx-mon3`: an invitation is never refused; `rtp_evicted` in `/api/status` counts the sessions ended to make room), `rtp_gaps` |
    | `rr` | `resync_requests` |
    | `ud` | `usb_in_discarded` |
    | `sr` | status requests answered, this reply included |
    | `hi` | free internal heap, bytes |
    | `rsn` | why the chip last reset: `poweron`, `sw`, `panic`, `task_wdt`, `brownout`, … (Self-recovery above) |
    | `bc` | boots since power-on, this one included: `1` after a power-on or RST |
    | `why` | what FlowLocal noted before it restarted itself: `cmd12`, `cmd13`, `web`, `bridge_hung`, `usb_wedged`; empty when nothing was noted |
    | `ur` | USB soft reconnects since this boot (the USB watch) |
    | `cs` | this boot's channel scan: `channel:score` for channels 1, 6 and 11, the scores in hundreds, rounded up (`0` only for a channel no network was heard near; lower is quieter; `cs=1:0,6:24,11:35` is `/api/status`'s `{"1": 0, "6": 2350, "11": 3425}`). Empty before the scan (the boot silence), when it failed, and with `FL_EXHIBIT_AP_CHANNEL` (no scan). It stays the same for the whole boot |

    A reply that would pass 240 bytes loses its last fields whole, never part of a value. `rsn`, `bc`, `why`, `ur` and `cs` come last on purpose, so they are the first to go, `cs` first: `cs`, `rsn`, `bc` and `why` do not change while FlowLocal runs, and right after a restart, when they matter most (a channel just chosen from the monitor, a restart FlowLocal did by itself), the counters are small and everything fits; a reader keeps the ones it got for that boot (`up` tells the boots apart). `cs` is shortened to hundreds because the reply is already about 220 bytes an hour into an ordinary day; even so it goes first once the counters grow (the host test's crowded hall: there at 239 bytes 40 s after the restart that set the channel, gone an hour later at 227 bytes up to `ur`). The restart keys fit as before: right after a restart with a lease at `.100`, all knobs at 127 and `why=bridge_hung` (237 bytes), only `cs` is left out; ten hours of a busy panel keep every key up to `ur` (236 bytes in the host test: 1.5 million messages each way; exactly 240 with an empty `cs`); the host test's busy day (238 bytes up to `hi`: a day of uptime, 10 million messages each way, a lease at `.100`) loses the five, and a month without a restart and every counter at its widest can also lose `ud`, `sr` and `hi`, which a reader then treats as unknown. A soft reconnect (`ur`) also shows on the laptop as the port leaving and returning while `up` goes on.
  - **Timing (`jt`, `jm`).** The panel stamps each RTP-MIDI packet with its media clock (AppleMIDI 3.5.0: 10 kHz, from its `millis()`, so in 1 ms steps). The bridge notes when it parses the packet, on the 64-bit `esp_timer` in the same units. `jt` is the RFC 3550 estimate `J += (|D| - J) / 16` over the change `D` in that delay from one packet to the next; `jm` is the spread of the delay over the last 50–60 s (six 10 s buckets). Messages the Wi-Fi holds up and then delivers in a bunch show as a large `jm` (in the host test, a second held and released: `jm=1000`, `jt` about 20 ms right after it). Both include this task's own delay (a pass of 1 ms, plus a reply being written) and drift by the two crystals' tolerance (a few ms per minute at most). A new panel session with another SSRC, or a clock jump of more than 10 s, starts both again; `jt` keeps its last value while the panel sends nothing, `jm` goes to `-1` after a quiet minute. Heartbeats do not count, only MIDI.
  - **Commands.** Each is logged (`[midi] pf monitor: command 10 (reconnect panel) ok: …`) and acked `cmd=<10|11|12|13>;ok=<0|1>;msg=<short English>`, e.g. `cmd=10;ok=1;msg=ended 1 RTP session, the panel invites again`. Each command runs at most once per 3 s (`FL_EXHIBIT_MON_CMD_MIN_MS`, each on its own clock); one sent sooner is refused (`ok=0;msg=too soon: once per 3 s, wait 2 s`), with one such ack per second per command at most and the rest only counted. A command with the wrong guard or length (13 needs exactly its one argument byte, the others none), or an unknown command byte, is not a command: it is discarded and counted in `ud`, and gets no ack.
    - **10 reconnect panel:** FlowLocal ends every RTP session it holds with a BY (the library's `sendEndSession()`, which also drops each from its list), so the panel sees its session end and invites again; with the exhibit panel's changes that is expected within a few seconds (not yet measured on hardware). For 1 s after it (`FL_EXHIBIT_RECONNECT_QUIET_MS`) the panel's packets still under way get no BY of FlowLocal's own: such a BY could reach the panel after it has started its next session, which carries FlowLocal's same SSRC. `ok=0` with no session to end, or before the hotspot is up.
    - **11 reboot panel:** a short task of its own (`fl-pf-http`, core 0, priority 2; never the bridge task) sends `POST /api/wifi/reboot` to port 80 of the panel's address, which is the newest session's, else the one hotspot station with a DHCP lease. With no session and none or several stations with a lease nothing is sent (`ok=0;msg=no panel session and 2 devices on the hotspot`). It waits up to 2 s to connect and 2 s more for the status line (`FL_EXHIBIT_PANEL_HTTP_MS`). `ok=1` only for HTTP 200 (the panel answers `{"ok":true,"rebooting":true}` and restarts 0.4 s later); otherwise the ack says what happened (`answered HTTP 404`, `no HTTP answer from panel …`, `no connection to panel …`). One request at a time (`busy`). The ack comes when the request is done, up to about 4 s after the command. The rebooted panel invites from the same address with a new SSRC, which replaces its old session here.
    - **12 reboot FlowLocal:** acked `ok=1`, then the board restarts 300 ms after the ack went out (or was given up; 2 s after the command at the latest). It comes back like any restart: USB and the monitor's replies within seconds, the hotspot after its 12 s silence and the channel scan, then the panel.
    - **13 Wi-Fi channel** (`F0 7D 50 46 13 43 48 <c> F7`): `<c>` `00` goes back to the automatic channel, `01`–`0D` fixes that channel, `7F` takes the next of 1, 6 and 11 after the channel the hotspot is on (1 → 6 → 11 → 1; one between them goes to the next one up, 3 → 6, one above 11 to 1). FlowLocal writes the choice to NVS (in the bridge task: a flash write of a few ms), acks `cmd=13;ok=1;msg=channel 11 from next boot, restarting now` (or `msg=auto from next boot, restarting now`), and restarts as for 12, with `why=cmd13`; the hotspot then comes up on the stored channel after the usual 12 s silence (see Hotspot channel above). Refused with `ok=0`, nothing stored and no restart: any other `<c>` (`msg=no channel 14: 0 auto, 1-13, 127 next of 1/6/11`; this refusal comes before the 3 s gate, so the corrected command need not wait), `7F` before the hotspot is up (`msg=no channel to go on from: the hotspot is still starting`), a build with `FL_EXHIBIT_AP_CHANNEL` (`msg=channel fixed at 6 by this build: nothing changed`), and NVS that cannot be written (`msg=could not save the channel (NVS): nothing changed`). The panel finds the hotspot on its new channel by itself (it scans every channel when it joins), usually within a minute (not yet measured on hardware).
  - **Rules.** At most one status reply per 200 ms: requests closer than that to the last reply, or sent while a reply waits, are counted (`status_unanswered`), not answered. The monitor asks every 2 s.
  - The bridge answers at the end of its 1 ms pass: after the panel's messages read in that pass, and after an owed re-send (Program Change, mirror, knob values), which always goes first; acks before a reply. A reply or ack that has waited 1 s behind a re-send, or has nobody to read it (no laptop, or writes failing for 0.5 s), is given up (`status_abandoned`, `monitor_acks_lost`). Up to 4 acks wait in a queue; one that finds it full is dropped (`monitor_acks_lost`).
  - Each reply or ack goes into the USB buffer whole, `F0` to `F7`, with nothing in between: another message's status byte would end the SysEx for the laptop. The bridge does nothing else while it writes one, which takes a few ms for about 200 bytes while the laptop reads (expected, not measured). If the buffer has not taken all of it within 20 ms, the rest is given up, and so is everything else waiting in that pass: the laptop is left with a SysEx without `F7`, which it drops when the next message arrives. The panel's messages wait in the Wi-Fi receive buffer meanwhile, 20 ms at most.
  - Replies and acks are not counted in `tx`, which counts MIDI messages only.
  - Every other SysEx from the laptop is discarded and counted in `usb_in_discarded`, one per USB packet: other manufacturers, the reply's and the ack's own shape, a request or command with more or fewer bytes, one longer than 8 bytes. A request cut short by another message (the resync request, for one) is discarded, and that message is handled as always. MIDI real-time bytes (`F8`–`FF`) inside a request do not cut it. Nothing from the laptop reaches the panel.
  - The hotspot values (`ap`, `ch`, `cl`, `prs`) come from the Wi-Fi driver and the DHCP server, which the bridge task does not call: `loop()` polls them every 500 ms (and `setup()` once as soon as the AP is up), so they can be half a second old. `prs` is sent only when the RSSI polled belongs to the current panel address.
  - Replies start about a second after FlowLocal boots, as soon as USB is up: through the hotspot's 12 s boot silence they say `ap=0`, an empty `ch` and `ps=0`, so a monitor can tell "FlowLocal is starting" (`ap=0` with `up` under about 15) from "the hotspot is down". Commands 10 and 11, and 13's "next", have nothing to act on then and are refused; 13 with a channel or "automatic" works (it restarts FlowLocal, silence included).
  - Every program that has the port open for input receives the replies and acks, Live included (Windows MIDI Services shares the port).
  - `FL_EXHIBIT_MON_FW_TAG` (1–32 printable characters without spaces, `;` or `=`; the build checks), `FL_EXHIBIT_MON_MIN_MS` (200), `FL_EXHIBIT_MON_WRITE_MS` (20), `FL_EXHIBIT_MON_HOLD_MS` (1000), `FL_EXHIBIT_MON_CMD_MIN_MS` (3000), `FL_EXHIBIT_MON_ACK_QUEUE` (4), `FL_EXHIBIT_MON_RESTART_MS` (300), `FL_EXHIBIT_RECONNECT_QUIET_MS` (1000, also the quiet time after the session BYs above), `FL_EXHIBIT_SESSION_TIMEOUT_MS` (25000, 21000–61000), `FL_EXHIBIT_PANEL_HTTP_PORT` (80) and `FL_EXHIBIT_PANEL_HTTP_MS` (2000) are compile-time.
- **Self-recovery** (added after the evening of 2026-09-25: after a re-plug FlowLocal restarted four times, then stayed on the laptop's USB list with its hotspot up but answered nothing over USB, in either direction, for over 10 minutes, until RST). The decisions are in `src/fl_recovery.h` and tested on the PC; the rest is in `src/fl_midi_bridge.h`.
  - **Why it restarted.** First thing at boot FlowLocal reads the chip's reset reason (`rsn`) and a small record in RTC memory that every reset except a power-on leaves alone: boots since power-on (`bc`) and the note FlowLocal writes just before it restarts itself (`why`). The record carries a magic word and a checksum; one that does not check out (power-on, or a brown-out deep enough to lose RTC memory) starts the count again at 1. On the S3 the **RST** button cuts the chip's power, so it reads as `rsn=poweron`, `bc=1`. The serial log has one line per boot: `[FlowLocal] boot 2 since power-on, reset reason sw, noted bridge_hung, automatic restarts 1 of 3`.

    | `rsn` | cause (ESP-IDF `esp_reset_reason()`) |
    |---|---|
    | `poweron` | power applied, or RST pressed |
    | `sw` | a restart in software (`why` says which, when FlowLocal asked for it) |
    | `panic` | a crash |
    | `int_wdt`, `task_wdt`, `wdt` | the interrupt watchdog, the task watchdog, another watchdog |
    | `brownout` | the supply dropped below the brown-out detector's level |
    | `pwr_glitch`, `cpu_lockup`, `deepsleep`, `sdio`, `usb`, `jtag`, `efuse`, `ext`, `unknown` | rare or not expected on this board |

    | `why` | FlowLocal restarted itself because |
    |---|---|
    | (empty) | nothing noted: a power-on, or a reset FlowLocal did not ask for (a crash, a watchdog, a brown-out, a firmware update from `/update`) |
    | `cmd12` | the PF monitor's **reboot FlowLocal** |
    | `cmd13` | the PF monitor's **Wi-Fi channel**, which stored a channel for this boot |
    | `web` | the status page's **Reboot** |
    | `bridge_hung` | the MIDI bridge task stopped (below) |
    | `usb_wedged` | USB stayed stuck after a soft reconnect (below) |
  - **Bridge watchdog.** The bridge task (`fl-midi`) subscribes to ESP-IDF's task watchdog (this core panics after 5 s) and feeds it every 1 ms pass; `loop()` also checks the task's pass count every 500 ms and restarts FlowLocal with `why=bridge_hung` after 3 s (`FL_EXHIBIT_BRIDGE_STALL_MS`) without a pass. Both, because neither sees everything: a task blocked for good is caught by `loop()` first, with a clear note; a task spinning on its core starves `loop()` (same core, lower priority), and then only the task watchdog's interrupt still runs: it restarts with `rsn=task_wdt`, and notes `bridge_hung` when the bridge's heartbeat is what stopped (a timeout of the web server's task or of core 0's idle task, which the task watchdog also watches, leaves `why` empty). `loop()` starts after `setup()`, so the 12 s boot silence and the channel scan never count; the bridge task runs its passes through them as always.
  - **USB watch (soft reconnect).** When the laptop's monitor keeps asking for status and no reply gets out whole for 8 s (`FL_EXHIBIT_USB_ASK_WINDOW_MS`; at least 3 requests, none more than 5 s after the one before) while the laptop has the device configured, FlowLocal takes itself off the USB bus for 0.5 s and back (`tud_disconnect()` / `tud_connect()`): the laptop sees the port leave and return and sets it up from scratch, which resets every USB endpoint on both sides. Logged (`[midi] usb watch: 5 status requests in 8 s, no reply got out: soft reconnect 1`) and counted (`ur`). The requests prove the laptop sends and that a program has the port open to read (the monitor asks only then), so no reply means what FlowLocal sends to the laptop (the USB IN endpoint) stopped moving; or TinyUSB missed the end of a USB suspend and refuses to send while the laptop is awake: the bridge now reads USB whenever the port is configured, suspended or not, to see those requests (and runs commands such as **reboot FlowLocal** then too). A whole reply is longer than everything FlowLocal can hold on its side (the build checks), so one that got out proves the laptop read it.
    - If no reply gets out within 10 s after the reconnect (`FL_EXHIBIT_USB_VERIFY_MS`) while requests come again, or the laptop never takes the device back, FlowLocal restarts with `why=usb_wedged`. If nobody asks after it, nothing more happens.
    - Soft reconnects that no reply follows wait longer each time: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, then 32 minutes apart (`FL_EXHIBIT_USB_RECONNECT_MIN_MS` doubled); a reply that gets out starts over.
    - **Nobody asking, no action**: Live closed, the monitor closed and a laptop asleep all look the same from FlowLocal's side, and all mean "leave USB alone". Only arriving status requests start it.
    - **Silent both ways** (nothing arrives from the laptop, nothing gets out) cannot be told apart on FlowLocal's side from nobody having the port open: a stuck bridge task is caught above, but a USB OUT endpoint that stopped taking the laptop's data, or a stopped USB controller, is not. Only the laptop can tell (its monitor gets no reply at all): press RST or re-plug.
  - **Restart limit.** Automatic restarts (`bridge_hung`, `usb_wedged`) stop after 3 since power-on (`FL_EXHIBIT_RECOVERY_RESTARTS_MAX`, counted in the RTC record): the checks then only log (`[boot] bridge_hung: no automatic restart left (3 since power-on, the most): press RST or replug`), and the bridge task does not subscribe to the task watchdog, so a fault that comes back after every boot cannot restart FlowLocal and its hotspot forever. RST or a re-plug (a power-on) gives them back. Restarts asked for by people (`cmd12`, `cmd13`, `web`) are not counted.
  - `FL_EXHIBIT_RECOVERY_RESTARTS_MAX` (3; 0 = never restart automatically), `FL_EXHIBIT_BRIDGE_STALL_MS` (3000), `FL_EXHIBIT_USB_ASK_WINDOW_MS` (8000), `FL_EXHIBIT_USB_ASK_MIN` (3), `FL_EXHIBIT_USB_ASK_GAP_MS` (5000), `FL_EXHIBIT_USB_OFF_MS` (500), `FL_EXHIBIT_USB_VERIFY_MS` (10000), `FL_EXHIBIT_USB_SETTLE_MS` (3000: a request after the reconnect counts as unanswered once it has waited this long) and `FL_EXHIBIT_USB_RECONNECT_MIN_MS` (60000) are compile-time; the build checks that they fit together.
- **Console:** `http://192.168.66.1/` (or `http://flowlocal.local/`) shows a status page with the panel session, when the panel was last heard, the laptop USB link, the current program, the program mirror, the knob values it would re-send (`CC 24-26 ch 1: 64 / 80 / –, 3 re-sent`, where `–` is a knob the panel has not sent) and counters. It also has the **Send current pattern to Live** button (it sends the knob values too), and hints for a full hotspot and for a panel still on a session from before a FlowLocal restart.
- **Panel address:** the status page shows `Panel: 192.168.66.x`, the address of the panel's RTP session, with links for staff on a phone joined to `PatternflowLocal`:
  - **Console** `http://<ip>/`, **MIDI** `http://<ip>/midi`, **Knobs** `http://<ip>/knobs`;
  - **Captions off** `http://<ip>/api/exhibit?caption=0` and **Captions on** `http://<ip>/api/exhibit?caption=1`.

  The address is the one the panel's invitation came from, recorded by the bridge task for the session it accepted; with two sessions, the newer one is shown.
  - **No session:** the card shows `Panel: unknown`, and these links are hidden. That is also when the panel's MIDI page is most needed: a panel with MIDI switched off, or with another computer saved as its host, never opens a session, so FlowLocal never learns its address. The card then lists the hotspot's other devices that have an address (not the phone viewing the page), each with **Console** and **MIDI** links. Which of them is the panel is not confirmed; the hotspot takes 3 devices, so there are at most two to try. With no other device listed, the panel is off or has not joined the hotspot.
  - **On the panel's MIDI page, every button, switch and slider saves to the panel as soon as it is tapped.** Only a typed host address waits for its **Save** button; everything else has no confirm step: Use this computer and Clear (host), Knob value / Relative steps, the sensitivity sliders and "move all four together", and MIDI on this panel. On the exhibit panel, Knob value / Relative steps and the sensitivity controls do not change what K1–K3 send: the panel sends each knob's absolute position whatever they say (`PF_MIDI_OUT_LANE_ABSOLUTE`, see Knob re-send above), and K4 never reaches MIDI. They still apply to relative knob clicks sent to the panel through its console API (`/api/params`), which the exhibit does not use. What a stray tap from a phone can break is the host (Use this computer, Clear) and MIDI on this panel: either one stops the panel inviting FlowLocal. Change nothing there unless you are repairing the setup. FlowLocal's status page says the same next to the links; the panel's MIDI page itself does not warn.
- **`GET /api/status`** returns the page's data as JSON. At the top level:
  - `panel_ip`: a string such as `"192.168.66.23"`, or `null` with no session;
  - `ap_stations`: the addresses of every device on the hotspot with a DHCP lease, the viewer and the panel included (e.g. `["192.168.66.2", "192.168.66.3"]`, empty with nobody on it). A device that has joined but has no lease yet is left out;
  - `viewer_ip`: the address the request came from, or `null`;
  - `ap_channel`, `ap_channel_mode` (`"auto"`, `"fixed"` or `"manual"`), `ap_channel_choice` (the monitor's command 13 choice read from NVS at boot: `0` automatic, 1–13; a fixed build shows it but does not use it) and, when a scan ran (automatic and manual), `ap_channel_scan`: `ok` (the scan finished), `ms`, `networks` and `scores` (`{"1": 0, "6": 2350, "11": 3425}`, exact; the reply's `cs` has them in hundreds);
  - `restart`: `reset_reason` (`rsn`), `restart_note` (`why`, `""` for none), `boots_since_power_on` (`bc`), `recovery_restarts` (automatic restarts since power-on) and `recovery_restarts_max` (3), e.g. `{"reset_reason": "sw", "restart_note": "bridge_hung", "boots_since_power_on": 2, "recovery_restarts": 1, "recovery_restarts_max": 3}`.

  The bridge fields are under `midi`:
  - `peers`: sessions with the handshake done, counted by SSRC;
  - `last_heard_age_ms`: time since the last MIDI or heartbeat; the panel sends a heartbeat every 10 s;
  - `usb_mounted`, `rx`, `tx`, `dropped`, `last_program`, `last_rx_age_ms`, `rtp_gaps`;
  - `resync_requests`, `web_resends`, `resent_program`;
  - `program_mirror` (`"CC 118 ch 16 value=program"`, or `"off"`) and `program_mirrors` (mirrors that reached the USB buffer, forwarded and re-sent together; `tx` counts them too);
  - `knob_resend` (`"CC 24-26 ch 1 value=last"`, or `"off"`), `knob_values` (K1–K3's last values, `null` for a knob the panel has not sent, e.g. `[64, null, 80]`; empty when off) and `knob_resends` (re-sent knob values that reached the USB buffer; `tx` counts them too);
  - `rtp_replaced`, `rtp_evicted` (sessions ended to make room for an invitation), `rtp_retries_answered` (repeated invitations that got their OK again), `rtp_timeouts` with `rtp_session_timeout_ms` (25000), `rtp_rejected` (always 0 from `flx-mon3`), `rtp_unknown`, `rtp_bye_sent`, `last_unknown_age_ms`;
  - the PF monitor's `status_request` (`"F0 7D 50 46 01 F7"`), `status_fw` (the `fw` tag), `status_requests` (requests received), `status_replies` (replies written whole), `status_unanswered` (requests within 200 ms of a reply or while one waited) and `status_abandoned` (replies given up);
  - its commands' `monitor_commands` (received with a valid guard), `monitor_commands_refused`, `monitor_acks` (written whole), `monitor_acks_lost`, `panel_reboots` (reboot requests the panel answered with 200) and `panel_reboot_path` (`"/api/wifi/reboot"`);
  - `rtp_jitter_ms` and `rtp_spread_ms` (`jt` and `jm` of the reply);
  - self-recovery: `usb_reconnects` (`ur`), `usb_watch` (`"watching"`, `"off the bus"`, `"reconnected, waiting for a reply"`), `bridge_stalled` (`loop()` has seen no bridge pass for 3 s; true only when no automatic restart is left, or for the moment before one) and `task_watchdog` (the bridge task is subscribed to the task watchdog);
  - and others.

  The status page shows the channel under Hotspot, how it was chosen and the scan's scores (`6 (set from the laptop monitor), busy at boot 1: 0 / 6: 2350 / 11: 3425`), and the timing as **Wi-Fi timing (jitter / worst, last minute)**. Under System it shows **Last restart** (`sw (bridge_hung), boot 2 since power-on`) and **Self-repairs (USB reconnects / restarts)** (`1 / 1 of 3`), and a stalled bridge task gives the hint "MIDI bridge is not running: reboot FlowLocal."

  `POST /api/resend` answers 409 until the panel has sent a Program Change. Otherwise it answers `program` and `knobs`, how many knob values go with it. `POST /api/reboot` restarts the board. `/update` is the browser OTA page from the regular build (arm it first).

## Recovery (expected from the code, not measured)

Every Program Change below is followed by its program mirror (CC 118, channel 16). Every one FlowLocal re-sends is also followed by the knob values it holds (CC 24–26, channel 1).

| Event | What happens |
|---|---|
| Panel restarts | It joins Wi-Fi and invites FlowLocal. The new session replaces the old one, and the panel sends its Program Change again (the exhibit panel, built with `PF_MIDI_OUT_LANE_ABSOLUTE`, also sends its K1–K3 values when a session starts, and FlowLocal keeps them). MIDI is back usually within a minute (not yet measured on hardware) |
| FlowLocal restarts (Reboot button, browser update, USB power loss) | The panel rejoins the hotspot. Its next heartbeat or knob message gets a BY, and it invites again at once. MIDI is back usually within a minute (not yet measured on hardware). The panel then sends its Program Change again, and USB (re)connecting makes FlowLocal send the last one it has, if any. A restarted FlowLocal holds no program and no knob values until the panel sends them |
| Live restarts | USB stays up, so FlowLocal sends nothing until Live asks. The Live device sends the resync request when it loads, and FlowLocal answers with the current Program Change and the knob values. The device's button, or **Send current pattern to Live** on the status page, does the same |
| Laptop sleeps and wakes | USB resume makes FlowLocal send the last Program Change and the knob values again. If the laptop cuts USB power in sleep, it counts as a FlowLocal restart |
| Monitor: reconnect panel | The panel's session ends with a BY; it invites again (with the exhibit panel's changes within a few seconds, not yet measured on hardware) and resends its Program Change and K1–K3 as on any new session |
| Monitor: reboot panel | As "Panel restarts" above |
| Monitor: reboot FlowLocal | As "FlowLocal restarts" above |
| Monitor: Wi-Fi channel | The choice goes to NVS, then as "FlowLocal restarts" above, with the hotspot on the new channel after the 12 s silence; the panel finds it there when it joins again (not yet measured on hardware) |
| Wi-Fi loses FlowLocal's OK to an invitation | The panel repeats the invitation about a second later and gets the same OK again; the session comes up on the repeat instead of after the panel gives up (not yet measured on hardware) |
| Both session slots taken when the panel invites (old sessions from other addresses) | The session heard from least recently is ended (a BY only to another address) and the panel's invitation accepted, never refused |
| Nothing heard from the panel's session for 25 s (heartbeats and MIDI lost, or the panel gone) | FlowLocal ends it with a BY (`to` one higher, `ps` 0): a panel still there invites again at once; one that is gone no longer holds a slot |
| The bridge task stops (blocked or spinning) | `loop()` restarts FlowLocal 3 s after the task's last pass, or the task watchdog 5 s after it; as "FlowLocal restarts" above. The replies say `why=bridge_hung` (`rsn=sw`, or `rsn=task_wdt`) |
| USB stops moving while the monitor asks (no reply gets out for 8 s), or TinyUSB missed the end of a suspend | Soft reconnect: the port leaves the laptop for about a second and returns; FlowLocal then sends the last Program Change and the knob values as on any USB (re)connect, and the replies go on with `ur` one higher. If replies still do not get out 10 s later: restart, `why=usb_wedged` |
| USB silent both ways (nothing from the laptop reaches FlowLocal) | FlowLocal cannot see it (above). The monitor gets no reply: press RST or re-plug the board |
| Automatic restarts used up (3 since power-on) | The checks only log; press RST or re-plug the board, which also gives them back |

## Build (Windows, Git Bash)

xtensa `ld` cannot write under a non-ASCII path, so keep the PlatformIO dirs on an ASCII path. The environment variables override `platforms_dir` / `packages_dir` in `platformio.ini`.

```bash
export PLATFORMIO_PLATFORMS_DIR=C:/Users/lsh/flx-pio/platforms
export PLATFORMIO_PACKAGES_DIR=C:/Users/lsh/flx-pio/packages
export PLATFORMIO_BUILD_DIR=C:/Users/lsh/flx-pio/build
python -m platformio pkg install -e exhibit      # first time only
python -m platformio run -e exhibit
python tools/check_exhibit_image.py              # must print PASS
```

The build writes `C:/Users/lsh/flx-pio/build/exhibit/firmware.bin` (the app) and `firmware.factory.bin` (bootloader, partition table, `boot_app0` and the app, flashed at `0x0`).

pioarduino installs its compiler (`toolchain-xtensa-esp-elf`, esp-14.2.0_20260121) with `idf_tools.py`, which refuses to run under Git Bash (`MSys/Mingw is not supported`) and downloads about 400 MB. ### Host test (no board)

```bash
python tools/check_exhibit_bridge.py             # must print PASS
```

It compiles the real `src/fl_midi_bridge.h` on the PC (GCC, Clang, or MSVC Build Tools found through `vswhere`) with its AppleMIDI session specialisations cut out, stand-ins for Arduino, FreeRTOS, Wi-Fi, UDP, NVS and TinyUSB (`tools/tests/exhibit_stubs.h`) and for the AppleMIDI front end (`tools/tests/exhibit_bridge_test.cpp`). ArduinoJson, AppleMIDI and the MIDI Library are the real ones from `.pio/libdeps/exhibit`, so run `pio pkg install -e exhibit` or one build first. The test runs the bridge task's own loop against a model USB host, a 16-packet buffer that the laptop empties every 1 ms while it reads, and checks, byte by byte and in order, what the laptop receives:
- the panel's messages pass through unchanged, and a pattern switch sends the Program Change and its mirror but no knob values;
- the resync request, the status page button, USB (re)connect and wake, and a dropped Program Change each send the Program Change, its mirror, then CC 24, 25 and 26 with the newest values;
- a knob the panel has not sent is left out, and nothing is re-sent before the first Program Change;
- only CC 24–26 on channel 1 are kept;
- a Program Change from the panel takes an owed one's place, and the knob values still follow it; a mirror owed on its own goes out without knob values;
- a re-send cut short by a full buffer finishes later in order without repeating anything;
- requests less than 250 ms apart count once;
- the `/api/status` fields;
- the PF monitor: a status request gets one whole reply, `F0` to `F7` with nothing between, after the panel's messages of the same pass and after an owed re-send; the reply's fields; at most one reply per 200 ms; a reply nobody reads is given up after 20 ms and the next message ends it for the laptop; other SysEx and a request cut short are discarded and counted, and the resync request works as before around them; a real-time byte inside a request does not cut it; `prs` only for the panel's own station; `ch` from the driver; replies before `begin()` (the boot silence) while RTP is not read yet;
- the PF monitor's commands: reconnect panel (a BY to every session the library holds, the ack with their count, no BY of FlowLocal's own for the panel's packets under way for 1 s), reboot panel (its own task, the exact `POST /api/wifi/reboot` it sends, the address from the session or the one station with a lease, the ack for HTTP 200, 404, silence, no connection, busy, no task), reboot FlowLocal (the ack, the restart 300 ms after it, also when the ack is given up or waits behind a re-send), Wi-Fi channel (the choice in NVS, the ack, the restart 300 ms after it with `why=cmd13` on the next boot; automatic, a channel, and "next" from each of 1, 6, 11, 3, 9 and 13; refused with nothing stored before the hotspot is up, when NVS cannot be written, and for arguments no channel can be, before the 3 s gate; another guard, no argument or two arguments are no command); once per 3 s each and one refusal ack a second; wrong guards discarded without an ack; acks before a reply and behind an owed re-send; `chm` and `cs` from the channel plan `setup()` publishes (once per boot); checks that compare a timestamp with the same pass's clock run on an even millisecond, where `millis() | 1` lies 1 ms ahead;
- `jt` / `jm` from RTP timestamps through `noteRtpTiming()`, in the reply and in `/api/status`;
- self-recovery: the boot line and `rsn`, `bc`, `why`, `ur` in the reply and `restart` in `/api/status`; the bridge task subscribing to the task watchdog once and feeding it every pass; `loop()`'s check quiet while the task passes and restarting 3 s after it stopped, with `why=bridge_hung` on the next boot; automatic restarts refused after 3 since power-on (and no task watchdog then), given back by a power-on; `cmd12` noted and not counted; the task watchdog's hook noting `bridge_hung` only for the bridge's own timeout and never over a restart already noted; the USB watch against a model laptop that stops reading, a suspend TinyUSB missed (requests read anyway, the resync request too), a bus reset that does or does not help, a laptop that does not take the device back, a monitor that stops asking, and 2 minutes each of nobody asking (awake, asleep, unplugged) and of a healthy laptop, where it never acts.

Then the RTP session test, `tools/tests/exhibit_session_test.cpp`: the whole `src/fl_midi_bridge.h`, its five specialisations included, compiled into the real AppleMIDI 3.5.0 and MIDI Library 5.0.2 over the same stand-ins, with a model UDP socket. A scripted panel sends what AppleMIDI sends as initiator (the invitation on both ports, heartbeats, RTP MIDI, BY), and the test checks what FlowLocal sends back (OK, BY, CK1; never NO) and its bookkeeping: a session joins with FlowLocal's SSRC and name in the OK; a repeated invitation gets the same OK, byte for byte, twice, then after the data invitation no answer, and the list still holds one session (a second device fits, a third makes it evict); a new token, or the same token from another port, replaces the half-made session without a BY; a rebooted panel (same address and port, new SSRC) replaces its session without a BY; a full list ends the session heard from least recently with a BY to its address and accepts the invitation, the evicted device's packets get no BY for 1 s and then one; one on the inviting address goes without a BY; heartbeats every 10 s and MIDI every 20 s keep a session, and 25 s of nothing ends it with a BY (not at 24.9 s), after which the panel invites again; a half-made session ends after 25 s; of two sessions on one address the silent one ends without a BY; the panel's own BY and **reconnect panel** go through the real library and clear the bookkeeping. Each of a dozen deliberate faults put back into a copy of the header (the stock library's ignored repeat and NO, the 61 s timeout, MIDI not counting as heard, a BY to the inviting address, no quiet time, the most recently heard evicted, …) makes it fail. zig's `c++` builds with the undefined-behaviour sanitizer, which stops at a reference the stock library binds to a field of its packed message structs (`getParticipantBySSRC(invitation.ssrc)`, as the firmware has run it since 2026-09-19); for this test only its alignment check is off.

Before the bridge it builds and runs three tests of pure headers, without stand-ins:
- `tools/tests/pf_monitor_test.cpp` against `src/fl_pf_monitor.h`: the SysEx assembler (requests, the four commands and their guards, command 13's argument byte, split by real-time bytes, other SysEx, strays, SysEx cut short, a status byte inside), the command gate (per command, across the clock's wrap), the reply text (key order, unknown values, `chm=manual`, `cs` in hundreds rounded up, ten hours whole up to `ur`, a busy day losing only the restart keys and `cs`, the restart keys whole right after a restart, `cs` there right after the restart that set a channel and gone an hour later, a month cut only between fields, every value at its widest within 240 bytes, the `fw` tag, `rsn` / `why` made fit, the shortest text at `STATUS_TEXT_MIN` bytes), the ack text, and the USB-MIDI packets for every text length from 0 to 240 bytes (CIN `0x4` for three bytes that start or continue it, `0x5` / `0x6` / `0x7` for the last one to three, unused bytes 0);
- `tools/tests/link_quality_test.cpp` against `src/fl_rtp_jitter.h` and `src/fl_ap_channel.h`: RFC 3550 steps, a steady link at 0, a bunched second (`jm=1000`), the window's 50–60 s, clock and `millis()` wraps, a new SSRC or a clock jump starting over; the channel weights and overlaps, the home scan picking 1, a loud network next door against several far away, HT40 secondaries at half weight, ties and invalid channels; the channel plan (a build's channel wins over NVS, 1–13 stored is manual, anything else automatic) and command 13's "next";
- `tools/tests/recovery_test.cpp` against `src/fl_recovery.h`: every `rsn` name and `why` note (`cmd13` not counted as automatic); the boot record (power-on and garbage counting from 1, the note reported by one boot only, automatic restarts counted, every flipped bit caught, counts that stop at their top); the heartbeat watch (a 1 ms task looked at every 500 ms never stalls, a stopped one is seen within 3–3.5 s, `millis()` and count wraps); the USB watch, a millisecond at a time: an hour of a healthy laptop and an hour each of nobody asking (awake, asleep, unplugged) never act, requests that stop at 4 s never act however long the silence, evidence from before a sleep does not count after it, fewer than 3 requests or gaps over 5 s never act, the reconnect at 8 s and what follows it (recovered, restart, the laptop not taking the device back, TinyUSB not noticing at all, nobody asking after it), the back-off of an hour of a wedge nothing mends (6 reconnects, 1 to 16 minutes apart), and all of it across `millis()`' and the counters' wraps.

No compiler on the PC? `pip install ziglang` provides one: point `CXX` at a one-line `zigcxx.cmd` containing `@python -m ziglang c++ %*`. (The compile-time guard checks compile with `-c` rather than `-fsyntax-only`, which zig's `c++` refuses.)

It runs the bridge test four times: as configured, with `FL_EXHIBIT_KNOB_RESEND=0`, with `FL_EXHIBIT_PROGRAM_CC=0` and with `FL_EXHIBIT_AP_CHANNEL=6` (`chm=fixed`, command 13 refused). Then it checks that the build refuses knob ranges containing CC 118 or 119, channel 16, and more than 8 numbers, self-recovery timings that do not fit together (a heartbeat limit as long as the task watchdog's 5 s, a single request as evidence, reconnects closer than one takes), and a session timeout of 20 s or 90 s. It does not cover the channel scan, the NVS read at boot and the AP start (`fl_wifi.h`), a real panel's library (the session test plays its part from the library's source), a real HTTP exchange with the panel, or real USB.

Do not add `lib_ignore` to this env. With `lib_ignore`, pioarduino strips IDF include paths out of the shared framework build script and restores them only after a successful link. A single failed exhibit build then breaks `env:flowlocal` (`diskio_impl.h` not found). This env gets its own entry point (`src/main_exhibit.cpp`) instead.

## Flash (COM jack)

Plug the **COM** jack into the flashing PC and find its port in Device Manager (`COM7` below). The laptop may stay on the USB jack.

Use one of these, and erase first either way. Erasing clears NVS (old AP settings) and `otadata`. If a board ever took a browser OTA into `app1`, writing only `firmware.bin` at `0x10000` would leave it booting the old app.

**PlatformIO** (erases the whole flash, then writes bootloader, partitions, `boot_app0` and the app):

```bash
python -m platformio run -e exhibit -t erase_upload --upload-port COM7
```

**esptool only** (esptool 5.x, e.g. `C:/Users/lsh/.platformio/penv/Scripts/esptool.exe`):

```bash
esptool --chip esp32s3 --port COM7 erase-flash
esptool --chip esp32s3 --port COM7 --baud 921600 write-flash 0x0 firmware.factory.bin
```

To wipe only NVS on a board that already runs this image, use `esptool --chip esp32s3 --port COM7 erase-region 0x9000 0x5000`.

There is no `uploadfs` step. This console is compiled into the app and does not use LittleFS.

## Check after flashing

1. Serial log on COM at 115200 shows:
   - `[midi] usb device "Patternflow Exhibit" started`
   - `[midi] usb watch: soft reconnect after 3 status requests over 8 s without a reply, restart if none gets out 10 s later`
   - `[midi] bridge watchdog: task watchdog on; loop() restarts after 3000 ms without a pass`
   - `[FlowLocal] boot 1 since power-on, reset reason poweron, automatic restarts 0 of 3` (after flashing, or RST)
   - `[FlWifi] AP starts in 12 s so a panel from before this boot lets go`
   - `[FlWifi] channel scan: … networks in … ms (…)` and `[FlWifi] channel scores ch1 …, ch6 …, ch11 … -> ch … (auto)`, about 9 s later
   - `[FlWifi] AP "PatternflowLocal" on 192.168.66.1 ch<n>, max 3 stations` with the channel just picked, and, on the first boot only, `[FlWifi] AP width 40 -> 20 MHz`
   - `[midi] rtp listener "FlowLocal" on 192.168.66.1:5004`
   - `[midi] program mirror: CC 118 ch 16 after every Program Change`
   - `[midi] knob re-send: CC 24-26 ch 1 after every re-sent Program Change`
   - `[midi] pf monitor: status request F0 7D 50 46 01 F7 answered with F0 7D 50 46 02 <text> F7 (v1, fw=flx-mon3, at most one per 200 ms)`
   - `[midi] pf monitor: commands 10 reconnect panel, 11 reboot panel (POST /api/wifi/reboot), 12 reboot FlowLocal, 13 Wi-Fi channel (kept in NVS, then a restart), each at most once per 3 s`
   - `[midi] rtp sessions: never refused (the one heard from least recently makes room), ended after 25 s unheard, a repeated invitation gets its OK again`
2. The laptop lists a MIDI port named **Patternflow Exhibit**. If Windows shows an old name, the board's VID:PID `303A:1001` is cached from an earlier firmware: uninstall the device in Device Manager and replug it.
3. Power the panel. The log shows `[midi] rtp session joined by "…"`, and the console reports the panel connected. The status page shows `Panel: 192.168.66.x`, and `panel_ip` in `/api/status` has the same address. From a phone on the hotspot, the **Console** link opens the panel's console.
4. Turn K4 on the panel. `last_program` changes in `/api/status`, and Live receives the Program Change followed by CC 118 on channel 16 with the same number. `program_mirrors` goes up by one.
5. Turn K1, K2 and K3. `knob_values` in `/api/status` shows the last values the panel sent (CC 24, 25, 26 on channel 1), and the status page shows them as `CC 24-26 ch 1: …`.
6. Press **Send current pattern to Live** on the status page. `web_resends`, `resent_program` and `program_mirrors` go up by one and `knob_resends` by three. Live receives the same Program Change and mirror again, then CC 24, 25 and 26 with the values in `knob_values`.
7. Load the Live set, or press the resync button on the Live device. `resync_requests` goes up, and Live receives the current Program Change, the mirror and the three knob values.
8. Close Live, turn K1 to the other end, and open Live again. After the set loads, the parameter mapped to K1 sits where K1 is (this depends on Live's Takeover Mode, see the last list below, and on the set not setting that parameter itself, see Knob re-send).
9. Start the laptop's PF monitor (or send `F0 7D 50 46 01 F7` to the **Patternflow Exhibit** output). A reply arrives every 2 s, the log shows `[midi] pf monitor: first status reply sent (… bytes)` once, and `status_replies` in `/api/status` goes up. `status_abandoned` stays at 0, and Live keeps playing as before. `ch` matches the log's channel, and `jt` / `jm` have values once the panel has sent MIDI.
10. From the monitor: **reconnect panel** (`F0 7D 50 46 10 52 43 F7`): the ack `cmd=10;ok=1;msg=ended 1 RTP session, …`, `ps` drops to 0 and comes back when the panel invites again. **Reboot panel** (`… 11 52 50 F7`): the ack `cmd=11;ok=1;msg=panel 192.168.66.x is rebooting`, the panel restarts and its session returns. **Reboot FlowLocal** (`… 12 52 46 F7`): the ack, then the port disappears and returns within seconds, and replies show `ap=0` until the hotspot is back, and `rsn=sw;bc=2;why=cmd12`.
11. The replies after flashing end with `rsn=poweron;bc=1;why=;ur=0;cs=…` (the scan's scores; left out of a reply that would pass 240 bytes). Press **Reboot** on the status page: they come back with `rsn=sw`, `bc` one higher and `why=web`. Press RST: `rsn=poweron;bc=1` again.
12. From the monitor, **Wi-Fi channel** "next" (`F0 7D 50 46 13 43 48 7F F7`): the ack `cmd=13;ok=1;msg=channel <n> from next boot, restarting now`, the restart, the log's `[FlWifi] channel <n> chosen from the laptop (PF monitor command 13, NVS)`, replies with `ch=<n>;chm=manual` and `why=cmd13` once the hotspot is back, and the panel's session back on the new channel. Then "automatic" (`… 13 43 48 00 F7`): `chm=auto` after the restart.

## Not verified on hardware

This build was compiled and its image scanned, but it has not run on a board. The following are untested:
- USB enumeration and the port name on Windows 11 and Live;
- the RTP session with the panel;
- latency;
- how Windows behaves while Live has the port closed (whether it drains the device);
- `softAP` station capping, and the one-second window with the driver's stored AP settings at boot;
- the session repairs and the recovery above (read from the AppleMIDI 3.5.0 source, not observed on the air); the recovery time is usually within a minute (not yet measured on hardware);
- the resync request coming from the Live device, on load and from its button;
- the program mirror reaching the Live device, and the device's Alt CC reading it as a program when Live does not deliver Program Change;
- the knob re-send reaching Live, and Live moving the parameters mapped to K1–K3 to the re-sent values. Live's Takeover Mode decides this: with **None** a mapped parameter takes each incoming value as it is; with **Pick-Up** or **Value Scaling**, Live may leave a parameter where it is when a single value arrives that differs from it (the case after a Live restart or a panel reboot). Which modes pass it has not been checked;
- the panel address on the status page, and whether a phone on the hotspot can open the panel's pages through its links;
- the list of hotspot devices the status page shows with no session (`ap_stations`), and whether the panel is among them;
- the PF monitor's replies through Windows MIDI Services while Live has the port open: how long a reply takes to leave the USB buffer, that Live ignores the SysEx it also receives, and what the laptop does with a reply given up halfway (a SysEx without `F7` followed by another message);
- `prs`, `ap`, `ch` and `cl` against what the panel and the hotspot report;
- the channel scan on the board: how long it takes, what it hears next to a laptop, and the STA-to-AP switch after it; the move from a stored channel to a new one right after the AP starts; the 40-to-20 MHz switch;
- the commands on the air: how soon the panel invites again after the BY of **reconnect panel**, the panel's answer to `POST /api/wifi/reboot` from FlowLocal, and the port coming back after **reboot FlowLocal**;
- the session changes of `flx-mon3` on the air (read from the AppleMIDI 3.5.0 source and tested against it on the PC, not observed): the panel taking a repeated OK, a list-full eviction, a session ended after 25 s unheard and the panel inviting again after its BY, and how often a lossy hall ends a live session that way (two heartbeats in a row lost);
- command 13 on the board: the NVS write from the bridge task, the stored channel after the restart, the driver starting the AP on it (and the fallback if it does not), and how soon the panel finds the hotspot on its new channel;
- `jt` and `jm` against a known link: the values here come from the host test only;
- self-recovery on the board: the RTC record across each kind of reset (a brown-out in particular, and whether a real one ends as `brownout` or as `poweron`), `rsn` after RST, the task watchdog's hook, the soft reconnect on Windows 11 (how the port leaves and returns for Windows MIDI Services, Live and the monitor, and how long the laptop takes to set the device up again), and whether any of it would have ended the wedge of 2026-09-25: its cause is not known, so which of the watches it would have woken, if any, is not either.
